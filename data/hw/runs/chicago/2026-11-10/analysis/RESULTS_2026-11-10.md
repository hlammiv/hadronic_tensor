## 2. It is a per-circuit effect, not an averaging effect

Centre kappa per twirl instance (15,000 shots, error 0.008 each):

| t | control2 | instance 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | pooled |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | +0.631 | +0.873 | +0.875 | +0.876 | +0.880 | +0.878 |
| 4 | +0.192 | +0.807 | +0.773 | +0.810 | +0.823 | +0.808 |
| 6 | +0.102 | +0.765 | +0.741 | +0.730 | +0.762 | +0.749 |

Every instance on its own beats the control by 0.25 to 0.65, and the four
agree to 0.02. So the gain is not the ensemble average cancelling a coherent
error between instances (which a commutant twirl could not do for a ZZ
over-rotation anyway, as Declan noted); each Pauli-dressed circuit is
individually a better mirror. The dressing merges random X/Y/Z Paulis into
the single-qubit layers around every rzz, which refocuses single-qubit
coherent errors (phase drift, ZZ crosstalk from idle neighbours) inside the
mirror block, where the Runtime's XY4 dynamical decoupling does not reach.
The undressed mirror block, rzz(1e-8) layers with no 1q gates between them,
is the worst case for such errors: nothing refocuses them for the whole
block. This is also why the raised mirror angle (8 Nov eps05) did not help:
it changed the two-qubit content, not the single-qubit refocusing.

Instance spread is larger at the odd sites (0.06 to 0.13 in the group
median; site 25 ranges 0.14 to 0.55 at t = 2) and at the next sites.

## 3. Independent check: the twirled mirror returns the matter Z profile

RMS over sites of <Z_n>(mirror t) - <Z_n>(t = 0 ref), no ancilla involved:

| run | t=2 | t=4 | t=6 | physics - ref (t=2/4/6) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| production 6 Nov | 0.185 | 0.351 | 0.446 | 0.30 / 0.36 / 0.41 |
| control 8 Nov | 0.225 | 0.309 | 0.353 | 0.28 / 0.33 / 0.38 |
| control2 10 Nov | 0.138 | 0.290 | 0.362 | 0.26 / 0.28 / 0.34 |
| twirl 10 Nov | **0.063** | **0.106** | **0.150** | 0.23 / 0.26 / 0.35 |

The untwirled mirrors return the profile no better than the physics
circuits move it; the twirled mirror returns it 2.5 to 3 times better at
every depth. At the odd sites the twirled mirror's residual is 0.04 to 0.06
against 0.07 to 0.16 for control2.

## 4. The odd sites are improved but not fixed

Odd-site median kappa 0.25 / 0.35 / 0.27 (twirl) vs 0.17 / 0.08 / -0.03
(control2). Positive at every t and above the 0.10 floor, so a twirled J1
run would retain its probes, but at a quarter to a third of the ideal
signal, with 3 to 4 times the error of the centre. Site 27 stays at 0.08 to
0.17; site 25 is 0.34 to 0.52 pooled with a wide instance spread. The
physics circuits' own damping at the odd sites is also poor (median 0.07 to
0.23 of ideal), so part of the odd-site problem is in the physics block,
not only the mirror.

## Consequences

- The Chicago mirror failure is a coherent single-qubit error inside the
  mirror block, cured by Pauli dressing at transpile time. `--pre-twirl 4`
  is the production setting for any further rzz run; the Runtime's own
  gate twirling is not available for fractional gates (error 1519).
- Four instances suffice: the instance-to-instance spread at the centre is
  0.02. More instances buy nothing at the centre; they would shrink the
  odd-site scatter.
- Proposed next hardware step (Henry's call, Declan runs it): a pre-twirled
  production pass on Chicago, J0 and J1 at all t, same cuts and
  `--min-t2 100`, so the W10 and the fast-particle components become
  measurable there. Estimated cost scales with the 6 Nov run times 1 (same
  shots, split into 4 instance jobs each).
- For the talk: the two-chip W00 replication stands as is (6 Nov data). The
  "finer components need the mirror fixed on Chicago" note now has an
  answer: the mirror is fixed by twirling; the data are not yet taken.
