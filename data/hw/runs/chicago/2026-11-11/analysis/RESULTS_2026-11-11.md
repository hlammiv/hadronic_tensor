## 2. The asymmetry of W00 does not replicate

Slow particle 1.10 vs 0.76 (difference 0.34 against a combined window
systematic of 0.27). Fast particle 0.09 vs 0.83, a clear failure. The maps
agree; the spatially odd part of G (G(24+d) - G(24-d), what A measures)
does not. Odd-part hardware/exact projection per t, relA: Phoenix 0.58 1.00
0.76 0.50 0.23 0.30 -0.14 0.09 0.15 0.05 0.39 -0.67 0.04; twirled Chicago
0.32 0.75 0.42 0.42 -0.21 -0.38 -0.04 -0.43 -0.73 -0.23 -0.19 -0.08 -0.15.
It goes negative from t = 2. A left-right difference around the insertion
is exactly what per-site calibration errors at the odd sites 21-27 corrupt
(10 Nov: odd-site kappa 0.25-0.35 after twirling, site 25 instance spread
0.14 to 0.55, site 28 masked at every t here). Not yet tested which sites
drive it.

## 3. W10 and W11 are blocked by a calibration rule, not the device

The J1-probe mirror kappa for each bond is sx_mirror / sx_ideal(0) with the
guard |sx_ideal(0)| > 0.02. On the production card only bonds 22, 25, 27
pass the guard (|sx_ideal0| = 0.11, 0.15, 0.06); bonds 23, 24 take the
prep-blind j0 Z-mirror kappa at site 24 (0.58-0.91 here, healthy); every
other bond gets the MEDIAN of bonds 22, 25, 27. Those three sit on the weak
odd sites, so the fill is near zero or negative at about half the times
(t = 2.25, 2.75, 3, 3.5-6) and 47 or 48 probes are masked; at the other
times (t = 3.25, most quarter times) the same fill lands above 0.05 and
nothing is masked. The far bonds' own qubits have mirror kappa ~0.7.
Proposed rule (Henry's call): fill far J1 bonds from the same-job j0
Z-mirror kappa at the bond's own sites times the probe-weight law, as the
prep-blind rule already does for bonds 23, 24.

## 4. Tests run 11 Nov evening (Henry: "test what you can now")

**Far-bond J1 fill rule** (`analyze_debias_j1fill.py`, TEST, not adopted):
anchorless J1 bonds take the same-job j0 Z-mirror kappa at their two sites
times exp(-0.1 t), error + 0.10 model. Control on Phoenix reproduces the
adopted numbers (slow W10 0.96 vs 0.94, fast W10 1.39 vs 1.39, fast W11
0.98 ± 0.05 vs 1.08 ± 0.12), so the rule is neutral where the mirror is
sound. On twirled Chicago (`test_j1fill/`) W10/W11 coverage rises to
0.95-0.98. Results: fast W11 0.93 ± 0.18 ± 0.05 (5.0σ), agrees with
Phoenix; fast W10 1.85 ± 0.08 ± 0.10 vs 1.39, disagrees; slow W10
2.88 ± 0.34 ± 0.15 vs 0.96, disagrees.

**Symmetric site drops** (`dropscan_w00_table.txt`): dropping the pairs
next to the insertion moves A on every device, Phoenix included (fast
particle 0.84 -> -0.08 with 23/25 dropped), so A lives in those sites.
Twirled Chicago stays below Phoenix for the fast particle under every drop
(sites 20-28 all dropped: 0.06 ± 0.07 vs 0.37 ± 0.12). The odd-site
calibration hypothesis is not supported: the deficit is not localized there.

**Raw shots**: the odd part of the UNcalibrated fast-particle correlator
(slice C, before kappa) projected on the exact odd part is positive on
Phoenix at t = 1.5-4 (0.57, 0.15, 0.89, 0.75, 0.62, 0.67) and negative on
twirled Chicago (-0.58, -0.28, -0.01, -0.06, -0.99, -0.43). Caveat: C still
contains the ideal one-point and vacuum terms (a common -0.7 to -0.9 offset
at t = 0 on all devices), so only the device difference is meaningful.
Reading: the wrong-sign boost is in the physics circuits' raw data, not
created by the calibration. A coherent ZZ over-rotation is a wrong
Hamiltonian (distorted propagation), which the mirror division cannot
correct by design and the ZZ-commutant twirl cannot randomize. Clean test:
mirror-diag + a W00 relA slice in the CZ basis on Chicago.

## 5. The even/odd investigation (12 Nov; scripts and logs in `evenodd/`)

Question (Henry): is the odd-site problem a coding bug, a layout effect, or a
gate-decomposition bias? Method: start from the t = 0 reference pubs (no
Trotter evolution) and from the vacuum-region sites, where the answer is
known, on all six datasets (Phoenix, Chicago 6, 8, 10 ctl, 10 twirl, 11).

**5.1 No estimator or parity bug.** Far from the insertion, <Z_n> at t = 0
is 0.80-0.86 of ideal on even AND odd sites on every device
(`t0_decomp.py`). Phoenix and both twirled Chicago runs stay parity
symmetric to 0.03 at every depth (`far_drift.py`).

**5.2 The untwirled parity asymmetry is a coherent error, not T1.**
Vacuum-site Z survival, physics circuit, t = 6: Chicago 6 Nov even 0.56, odd
0.20 (Phoenix 0.27/0.29, twirled Chicago 0.45/0.46). But the sign flips with
layout and depth (10 Nov control mirror: even 0.38, odd 0.60; 8 Nov mirror
non-monotonic), T1 is 180 us, and the loss does not track 1/T1
(`t1_check.py`). A coherent error imprints by sublattice because the Trotter
circuit is sublattice-periodic. Twirling removes it. Solved for twirled data.

**5.3 The "ancilla X bias" is the prepared state's <Z_24>.** At t = 0 the
ancilla X outcome equals the insertion site's Z outcome shot by shot
(c_24 = 0.9), and <X_anc> = <Z_24>_hw in all eight t = 0 pubs: -0.019/-0.024
(Phoenix), +0.057/+0.062 (6 Nov), -0.023/-0.025, -0.004/-0.004,
-0.068/-0.070, -0.058/-0.060, -0.094/-0.096. The de-bias rule is still the
right operation (it makes the connected subtraction use the hardware
one-point), but RESULTS_2026-11-09's reading "a property of the ancilla
qubit" is wrong: it is a preparation error at the insertion site.

**5.4 The packet is poorly prepared on every device.** The connected
ancilla-site correlator at t = 0 is 0.88-0.93 of ideal at the centre and
0.3-0.7 at sites 22, 23, 25, 26 on Phoenix and on every Chicago run, even
and odd alike. Not Chicago-specific.

**5.5 The mirror understates the physics circuit's loss on every device.**
Vacuum-site Z survival mirror vs physics at t = 6: Phoenix 0.50 vs 0.28,
twirled Chicago 0.78 vs 0.45; ratio 0.55-0.58 on both. Near the insertion
the raw physics correlator at sites 25, 27 is ~0.05 of ideal at t = 4 on
Phoenix too (-0.01 vs -0.43, -0.01 vs +0.31), where the mirror keeps
0.4-0.7. What survives calibration off-centre is similar on Phoenix and
twirled Chicago (table in the session log); the maps agree because the
centre dominates.

**5.6 The 10/11 Nov layout puts the packet's flanks on bad hardware**
(`gauss.py`, `layout_quality.py`). Gauss syndromes at t = 0: sites 27, 28
read 0.11-0.14 and 22, 23 read 0.2-0.4 (0.65 elsewhere). Link 27 = q30:
T2 23 us, couplers 1.4/1.7/4.5% (median 0.25%), <X_link> = -0.09. Sites 27,
28 (q32, q31): couplers 1.7-1.9%, readout 2.6-4.0%. Site 26 (q33): readout
4.8%. Insertion-to-25 bond q53-q43: 0.77%. Site 22 (q51): T2 57 us. All
pass the cuts (2% two-qubit = 8x median; T2 floor covers 6 core qubits).
Left and right flanks are damaged differently, which is what a left-right
asymmetry measures. Every Chicago layout so far has had defects in the
packet region (6 Nov: site 23 on T2 22 us; 8 Nov: centre on T2 70 us).
A device-wide cut cannot fix it: at 4x median no Ns = 50 ladder exists on
the 11 Nov calibration (`cuts_scan.py`); at 6x the ladder moves to q102
with a T2 29 us qubit in the packet region.

**5.7 Four twirl instances are not enough, and the quoted errors omit it**
(`instances.py`, `../instances/`). The prepared state differs between twirl
draws far beyond shot noise (0.008): <Z_22> 0.15-0.42, <Z_24> -0.02 to
-0.14 across the 4 instances of one pub. A physics pub and its mirror draw
different twirls. Single-instance reductions of the whole run:

| | pooled (quoted shot err) | instance 0, 1, 2, 3 | scatter / 2 | Phoenix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| slow W00 | 1.10 ± 0.04 | 0.54, 1.06, 1.03, 1.24 | 0.15 | 0.76 |
| fast W00 | 0.09 ± 0.11 | 0.28, 0.07, 1.07, 0.44 | 0.22 | 0.83 |
| fast W10 | 1.89 ± 0.10 | 1.57, 1.52, 1.46, 1.85 | 0.09 | 1.39 |

With the empirical error the slow particle is 1.10 ± 0.15 ± 0.23 against
0.76 (consistent); the fast particle is 0.09 ± 0.22 against 0.83 (3 sigma
low, one instance alone gives 1.07 ± 0.15). Phoenix's Runtime twirl uses 32
randomizations per pub (Runtime default; Chicago metadata confirms 32 for
its measurement twirl; Phoenix metrics are not local).

**Status of earlier hypotheses.** "Odd-site calibration" (sec. 2): replaced
by 5.6 + 5.7. "RZZ over-rotation distorts the dynamics" (sec. 4): not
needed to explain the data and still untested.
